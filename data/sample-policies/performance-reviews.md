Title: Performance Reviews and Promotion
Category: Career
Owner: People Operations
Effective: 2026-01-01

## Cycle

Meridian runs one formal performance review per year, covering the calendar year
and completed in February. A lighter mid-year check-in happens in July and does
not produce a rating.

Employees who joined after October 1 are not rated in the following February
cycle; their first review is the year after. New hires instead complete the
90-day check-in described in the New Hire Onboarding Guide.

## What the review covers

Reviews assess two things separately: what you delivered, and how you worked with
others. Both are rated, and both count.

Ratings are: Below Expectations, Meets Expectations, Exceeds Expectations, and
Outstanding. Most employees performing well receive Meets Expectations — it is
the expected outcome, not a warning sign.

Meridian does not use forced distribution or stack ranking. Managers are not
allotted a quota of high or low ratings.

## Inputs

The review draws on your self-assessment, your manager's assessment, and peer
feedback from three to five colleagues. You nominate peers; your manager may add
one. Peer feedback is shared with you in summarized form, not attributed.

Write the self-assessment. It is the primary record of work your manager may not
have seen directly, and it carries real weight.

## Calibration

Before ratings are final, managers within an organization meet to compare how
they are applying the scale. Calibration adjusts for managers who rate
systematically high or low. Your manager delivers the final rating and must be
able to explain any change made in calibration.

## Compensation

Merit increases are effective April 1 and are tied to rating and position in
band. Bonus, where your role has one, is paid in March based on company
performance and individual rating.

A rating is not a promise of an increase. In a year where the company does not
fund merit increases, ratings still occur.

## Promotion

Promotions are considered twice per year, in February and August. Promotion
requires that you are already operating at the next level consistently — it
recognizes a change that has happened, rather than granting a trial.

Your manager submits a promotion case with specific evidence. Cases are reviewed
by directors across the organization together, so the bar is applied consistently.

If a case is declined, your manager must tell you what specifically was missing
and what would change the outcome.

## Underperformance

A Below Expectations rating leads to a documented improvement plan of 60 to 90
days with specific, measurable goals and a scheduled check-in cadence. People
Operations reviews every plan before it is delivered.

The purpose of a plan is improvement. Employees who meet the goals return to
normal standing with no lasting record beyond the cycle.

## Disagreeing with a review

Raise it with your manager first. If unresolved, request a review by your
manager's manager with People Operations present. Requests must be made within 30
days of receiving the rating.
