Title: Conflict of Interest Policy
Category: Conduct & Compliance
Owner: Legal
Effective: 2026-01-01

## Policy statement

A conflict of interest exists when your outside activities, relationships,
or financial interests could reasonably influence, or appear to influence,
your judgment on behalf of Meridian. Most conflicts are manageable once
disclosed; failing to disclose is what turns a manageable situation into a
violation.

## Common conflicts

- **Outside employment.** Work for a competitor, customer, or vendor, or a
  side business in the same industry as Meridian.
- **Financial interest.** Owning more than 1% of a private competitor,
  customer, or vendor, or any equity received as compensation from one.
  Publicly traded securities held as part of a normal diversified
  investment are not a conflict unless you hold more than 1%.
- **Hiring or supervising a relative.** A relative or someone you have a
  close personal relationship with reporting to you, directly or
  indirectly, or being on a hiring panel that evaluates them.
- **Vendor selection.** Choosing or influencing the choice of a vendor in
  which you or a close relative has a financial interest.
- **Board service.** Serving on the board of another company, especially a
  competitor or potential acquisition target.

## Disclosure

Disclose a potential conflict to your manager and Legal as soon as you
become aware of it, using the form at meridian.io/coi-disclosure. Legal
reviews disclosures within 10 business days and responds with one of three
outcomes: no conflict, manageable with conditions (such as recusal from a
specific decision), or a change is required.

Annual disclosure is required of all employees at the start of each year,
even if there is nothing new to report.

## Reporting relationships

A relationship within a direct or indirect reporting line, romantic or
close personal, must be disclosed to People Operations so the reporting
line can be adjusted. See the Code of Conduct for the process; the more
senior person in the relationship is responsible for raising it.

## Gifts and vendor relationships

See the Anti-Bribery and Gifts Policy for limits on what you can accept
from a vendor, customer, or partner while a conflict-relevant relationship
or purchasing decision is active.

## Consequences

Failing to disclose a known conflict is treated as a policy violation
independent of whether the conflict caused actual harm, and is addressed
under the Disciplinary Action and Termination Policy. Timely disclosure,
even of a significant conflict, is not itself a violation.
